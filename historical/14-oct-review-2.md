## Summary, highest priority first

1. **C-1 (likely, high impact).** A remapped queued tab can block or mark a profile other than the queued one. Nothing returns after the remap is detected.
2. **S2-1 (likely, high impact).** The enqueue single-flight lapses in the normal startup window. The reset threshold is 250 ms, and runtime emptiness alone triggers it.
3. **S5-1 (confirmed, high impact).** A fill timeout clears the in-flight marker, but the abandoned fill loop keeps opening tabs. Overlapping fills follow whenever a fill runs past 20 s, which a multi-tab fill can do. How often that happens in practice is not measured.
4. **S3-2 (likely, medium).** Cancel and clear delete rows chosen from an earlier read. The door applies removals without checking the row's attempt.
5. **S1-1 (likely, medium).** A scoped progress subset overwrites the module-level progress cache. That cache is also the fallback merge base in `normalizeProgressPatch` (S4-3).
6. **S1-2 (confirmed, medium).** The materialization decision tree is written twice, and the copies have already diverged.
7. **S4-1 (confirmed, low to medium).** Body-supplied tab ids take precedence over the authenticated sender, and the stale-sender branch closes tabs by that id.
8. **S3-1 (likely, medium).** A shared pause flag is cleared unconditionally by cancel and clear, and the fill loop reads it on every iteration.
9. **C-2 (likely, low to medium).** A claim that arrives while the tab is busy is accepted with `accepted: true`, and it is not run when the action ends.
10. **C-5 (confirmed, medium).** The progress merge exists in three places: the worker, `content.js`, and the per-tab cache that both use as their base.

Systemic note: S2-1, S2-2, S5-1 and S3-1 share one shape. A timeout or stop signal is used, but the work it times out keeps running and keeps writing to module-level state. One cancellation token passed through the enqueue, fill and reconcile paths would address the whole group.

### Slice S1: `runCoordinatorExecutorApplyPhase` (sw.js:18069–20712, 2,645 lines) — read in full

**S1-1. Likely, medium. A scoped progress subset overwrites the module-level progress cache.** The apply pass sets `profileNukeProgress = progress` ([sw.js:18214](../shared/js/sw.js:18214)) unconditionally. When the fast-path runtime-recovery pass runs with `scopedProgressForQueueOnly` ([sw.js:22518](../shared/js/sw.js:22518)), `progress` is only the queue's own hrefs ([sw.js:18190-18201](../shared/js/sw.js:18190)). The global is then a partial map. Later, `pruneQueuedTabActions` reads it on the non-queue-only path ([sw.js:30619](../shared/js/sw.js:30619)), and gets `null` for any href outside that subset. Two related points:
- The non-queue-only branch also falls back to the global when authority progress is empty ([sw.js:18200-18204](../shared/js/sw.js:18200)). That reintroduces a second, unscoped copy of progress as a source of truth.
- A third assignment, inside `mutateCoordinatorFailedItemsRetry` at [sw.js:23241](../shared/js/sw.js:23241), sets the global from the full runs-events-progress read.

The global has no stated invariant about which hrefs it covers. This is the storage-abstraction leak in entropy pattern 7. Suggested fix: stop assigning scoped subsets to the global, and have `pruneQueuedTabActions` read through the coordinator reader.

**S1-2. Confirmed, medium (maintainability and drift). The materialization decision tree is written twice.** The first copy handles the per-item loop's outcomes ([sw.js:19939-20155](../shared/js/sw.js:19939)). The second handles the consumed-keys loop ([sw.js:20156-20360](../shared/js/sw.js:20156)). They have diverged in small ways. For example, the enqueue-failure error text mapping for the fill-timeout and stale-pruned cases appears only in the second copy ([sw.js:20386-20393](../shared/js/sw.js:20386)). The `QUEUED_TAB_CREATE_TIMEOUT_MS * 6` retry delay appears only in the first ([sw.js:20083](../shared/js/sw.js:20083)). One decision table, shared by both loops, would remove the drift.

**S1-3. Confirmed, low to medium (contract by string). Control flow depends on free-text reason strings.** The requeue branch matches `restoreResult.reason` with a regex ([sw.js:18598](../shared/js/sw.js:18598)). I verified the producers exist ([sw.js:16705](../shared/js/sw.js:16705), [16714-16715](../shared/js/sw.js:16714), [16796-16797](../shared/js/sw.js:16796)), so it works today. A rename in either place would silently stop requeueing. The producer at [sw.js:16781](../shared/js/sw.js:16781) (`active-live-runtime-tab-closing`) does not match the regex. Confirm that falling through to "unresolved" is intended. This is entropy pattern 6, duplicated literals across a boundary.

**S1-4. Confirmed, low to medium (pattern 8, timing vocabulary).** The apply phase contains 72 `scheduleCoordinatorQueueResume` and `scheduleCoordinatorReconcile` calls, and 64 of them pass a bare literal delay (1000, 500, 250, or 100). The meaning of each delay is not named, and nothing relates them to the budgets they are meant to fit inside. The `* 6` on `QUEUED_TAB_CREATE_TIMEOUT_MS` ([sw.js:20083](../shared/js/sw.js:20083)) is an unexplained multiplier.

**S1-5. Likely, low. Fire-and-forget fills that swallow their errors.** The no-open re-prime and the stale-action re-prime call `void fillQueuedTabs(...).catch(() => null)` from `setTimeout(..., 100)` ([sw.js:20634-20646](../shared/js/sw.js:20634), [20674-20687](../shared/js/sw.js:20674)). Unlike the adopted-tab path at [sw.js:19324-19334](../shared/js/sw.js:19324), no diagnostic is recorded. A failure in those paths is invisible.

**S1-6. Question, low. A timeout result reports `changed: true` without a change.** When the enqueue times out and some cases were already consumed, the function returns `changed: true` ([sw.js:19599-19612](../shared/js/sw.js:19599)). If nothing new changed in this pass, callers that loop on `changed` could re-run it needlessly. Confirm the intent.

**S1-7. Question, carried forward to S2.** The enqueue is wrapped in `promiseWithTimeout` ([sw.js:19513](../shared/js/sw.js:19513)). A timed-out enqueue keeps running in the background, and the timeout branch then mutates state ([sw.js:19529-19537](../shared/js/sw.js:19529)). Check whether a late completion of the enqueue can overwrite that state. See slice S2.

**S1-8. Readability and cosmetic.** An empty `if` block with no body at [sw.js:18118-18119](../shared/js/sw.js:18118). Mis-indented result fields at [sw.js:19457-19458](../shared/js/sw.js:19457) and [20702-20703](../shared/js/sw.js:20702). Nested ternaries for `reason` at [sw.js:20708-20710](../shared/js/sw.js:20708) and for the record selection at [sw.js:18475](../shared/js/sw.js:18475). `finishCoordinatorExecutorApplyResult` is called 22 times with near-identical objects, which is the same drift risk as S1-2.

The row-changed guard in the terminal settlement compares the row before it applies the change ([sw.js:18512](../shared/js/sw.js:18512)). ### Slice S2: executor start latch and enqueue (sw.js:12652–12700, 14429–14720 of 14993) — read in part

**S2-1. Likely, high impact. The single-flight guard for the enqueue lapses in the common startup window.** `enqueueExistingExecutorForCoordinator` serializes enqueues with a promise chain. It treats the in-flight start as detached, and resets the chain, when `runtimeSurfaceEmpty || detachedLatch`, `pendingCount > 0`, and `ageMs >= COORDINATOR_DETACHED_EXECUTOR_START_PROMISE_STALE_MS` ([sw.js:12657-12667](../shared/js/sw.js:12657)). That threshold is 250 ms ([sw.js:800](../shared/js/sw.js:800)). `detachedLatch` is itself "no active tabs, no pending actions, no visible fill" ([swStorageIdlePromiseLatch.js:286-289](../shared/js/swStorageIdlePromiseLatch.js:286)), so the reset condition is effectively "runtime empty and older than 250 ms". An enqueue is typically in that state before its first tab opens, and it can spend longer than 250 ms in `pruneQueuedTabActions` and `sweepStaleQueuedTabs` alone ([sw.js:14436-14443](../shared/js/sw.js:14436)). I did not measure the durations. The reset itself, [sw.js:12385-12388](../shared/js/sw.js:12385), replaces the chain head with `Promise.resolve()`, so the old enqueue keeps running detached and a second one starts beside it. This is entropy pattern 4 (reset racing in-flight work).

The protection being lost is the one the enqueue relies on. It writes the module-level `queuedTabConcurrency` ([sw.js:14480-14482](../shared/js/sw.js:14480)), and it dedupes planned actions against a snapshot taken at the start ([sw.js:14488](../shared/js/sw.js:14488), [14617](../shared/js/sw.js:14617)). Two overlapping enqueues can clobber the first value and plan the same action twice. Suggested fix: decide "stuck" from the in-flight promise's own state or from a threshold far above the apply timeout, not from runtime emptiness. A test should drive two enqueues across the 250 ms boundary and assert one plan per action.

**S2-2. Likely, medium. The apply-phase timeout does not cancel the enqueue it times out.** `promiseWithTimeout` at [sw.js:19513](../shared/js/sw.js:19513) returns control on timeout, but the underlying `enqueueExistingExecutorForCoordinatorOnce` keeps running. Its later writes are then not fenced against the timeout branch that already applied `EXECUTOR_ENQUEUE_TIMEOUT` ([sw.js:19529-19537](../shared/js/sw.js:19529)). Together with S2-1, a late completion can land after the next enqueue has begun. This is the carry-forward from review point S1-7.

**S2-3. Likely, medium. A `...Locked` function is called without its lock.** `settleTerminalCoordinatorItemsLocked` is documented as running inside the space-feed storage lock. Its public wrapper takes that lock ([sw.js:11845-11848](../shared/js/sw.js:11845)). The reconcile caller is wrapped ([sw.js:11087](../shared/js/sw.js:11087)). The two calls inside the enqueue loop ([sw.js:14579](../shared/js/sw.js:14579) and [14610](../shared/js/sw.js:14610)) are not. `enqueueExistingExecutorForCoordinatorOnce` is called directly from three sites ([sw.js:19493](../shared/js/sw.js:19493), [21232](../shared/js/sw.js:21232), [22619](../shared/js/sw.js:22619)), and I did not find a lock around any of them. I could not rule out a lock higher in the call chain, so this needs confirming. If confirmed, the settlement writes can interleave with other storage updates, which is what the lock exists to prevent.

**S2-4. Confirmed, low. Concurrency state kept in module globals.** `queuedTabConcurrency` is a module-level value written per enqueue call ([sw.js:14480](../shared/js/sw.js:14480)). Its correctness depends on S2-1 holding, and nothing else protects it.

**S2-5. Confirmed, low. A timing magic number with no name.** The plan loop uses `COORDINATOR_EXHAUSTED_ROLLBACK_TERMINATE_BATCH_LIMIT` as its batch cap, which is named. The apply phase's `* 6` and the 250 ms and 1500 ms latch thresholds ([sw.js:799-800](../shared/js/sw.js:799)) are not explained in relation to each other or to the 180 s apply timeout ([sw.js:794](../shared/js/sw.js:794)). This is pattern 8, with the same failure shape as the nested-budget example in the entropy plan (pattern 5).

**Verified sound in S2:** the coalescing branch returns a coherent result and schedules a resume rather than dropping the work ([sw.js:12664-12678](../shared/js/sw.js:12664)). The first enqueue's exhausted-rollback handling checks the per-tick batch limit before doing work ([sw.js:14553](../shared/js/sw.js:14553)).

### Slice S3: pause, cancel and clear (sw.js:21606–21844, 25771–25911, door at 9782–9790) — read in part

**S3-1. Likely, medium. A shared pause flag is cleared unconditionally by cancel and clear.** `coordinatorPauseRequested` is one module-level boolean. Pause, cancel and clear each set it, and the cancel and clear `finally` blocks clear it unconditionally ([sw.js:21761](../shared/js/sw.js:21761), [21841](../shared/js/sw.js:21841)). If a user pause runs while a cancel or clear is in progress, the cancel's `finally` clears the flag while the user's pause is still persisting. The flag is the stop condition of the fill loop's hot path ([sw.js:34567](../shared/js/sw.js:34567)). The storage write of `false` is generation-checked, which limits the damage on disk. The in-memory guard has no generation check, so the fill loop can keep opening tabs during a pause. Suggested fix: give the local guard a generation, or make cancel and clear restore the previous value instead of forcing `false`.

**S3-2. Likely, medium. Removals in cancel and clear are decided from a read taken before the write, and the door applies them without a check.** `mutateCoordinatorStoredWorkClear` reads the queue through `coordinatorQueueReader.runsEvents` ([sw.js:25778-25783](../shared/js/sw.js:25778)), builds `removedRows` from that read, and passes them to `persistCoordinator`. The door's `applyCoordinatorQueueChange` deletes each `removedRows` key unconditionally ([sw.js:9782-9790](../shared/js/sw.js:9782)). It does not compare `previousRow`, and it does not check an attempt id. The per-row mutation path does check (`expectedAttemptId` and `context.reject`, [sw.js:3928-3930](../shared/js/sw.js:3928)). The removal path does not. A row that became active with a new attempt between the read and the write is deleted, while its newly opened tab is absent from `ownedTabIds`, so `stopOwnerNukes` does not close it. The tab then holds an executor slot until the orphan sweep finds it (see the owned-tab decision in the layering plan, section 13). Interacts with S2-1: an in-flight enqueue is not fenced by the pause, so it can start rows after the read.

Suggested fix: carry `expectedAttemptId` on each removal, and have the door skip any removal whose current row no longer matches, the way the per-row path already does.

**S3-3. Confirmed, low. Cancel and clear are near-duplicates.** `cancelCoordinatorCurrentWork` and `clearCoordinatorCurrentWork` ([sw.js:21687-21764](../shared/js/sw.js:21687), [21771-21844](../shared/js/sw.js:21771)) repeat the same pause, stop, close, prune and finally sequence, with the same error handling. A fix to one (such as S3-1) has to be made to both by hand.

**S3-4. Confirmed, low. Silent tab-close failures.** Owned tabs are closed in a loop with `catch {}` ([sw.js:21729-21736](../shared/js/sw.js:21729), [21813-21820](../shared/js/sw.js:21813), [21641-21646](../shared/js/sw.js:21641)). A tab that cannot be closed leaves no record. Combined with S3-2, that is an orphan with no diagnostic.

**Verified sound in S3:** the pause tombstones progress on delete ([sw.js:24718-24728](../shared/js/sw.js:24718), used at [24784](../shared/js/sw.js:24784)), which is the fix for entropy pattern 4 on the progress side. Cancel and clear take a fresh pause generation from storage when they start, and the pause-state writer rejects any write whose generation is older than the stored one ([sw.js:21689](../shared/js/sw.js:21689), [21775](../shared/js/sw.js:21775), and the check in `mutateCoordinatorPauseStateSet` at [sw.js:21864](../shared/js/sw.js:21864)). So a stale clear cannot overwrite a newer pause on disk. The in-memory flag in S3-1 has no such check.

### Slice S4: row events and progress merge (sw.js:32681–32940; swProfileProgressMerge.js:314–400; swProfileProgressMerge.js:449; swCoordinatorProgressRules.js:143; swCoordinatorRowEventMetrics.js:60) — read in part

**S4-1. Confirmed, low to medium (input precedence). Body-supplied tab ids take precedence over the authenticated sender.** Three places derive the sender tab from the message body:
- `normalizeCoordinatorRowProgressEvent` uses `event.senderTabId`, then `event.tabId`, then `event.patch.tabId` ([swCoordinatorRowEventMetrics.js:69](../shared/js/swCoordinatorRowEventMetrics.js:69)).
- `addSenderTabIdToProgressPatch` uses `patch.tabId` before the authenticated `senderTabId` ([swProfileProgressMerge.js:450](../shared/js/swProfileProgressMerge.js:450)).
- `getActiveCoordinatorProgressSenderTabMismatchReason` uses `options.senderTabId || patch.tabId` ([swCoordinatorProgressRules.js:146](../shared/js/swCoordinatorProgressRules.js:146)). On the deferred-flush path, `options.senderTabId` can be null, so the body id is what gets checked.

The stale-sender branch then closes whatever tab that id names: `closeRejectedStaleRowEventSenderTabs` calls `browser.tabs.remove` on `event.senderTabId` ([sw.js:32698](../shared/js/sw.js:32698), set at [sw.js:32833](../shared/js/sw.js:32833)). The exposure is limited, because the body is built by the content script and the page cannot send runtime messages. Still, a content-script defect could close an unrelated tab, and the check would pass on a body id that matches the item's own tab. Suggested fix: take the sender tab only from `sender.tab.id` at the message boundary, and remove the body fallbacks.

**S4-2. Confirmed, medium (entropy pattern 0, the root pattern). The progress merge is still a spread over one unioned record.** `normalizeProgressPatch` builds `next = {...previous, ...cleanPatch, ...}` ([swProfileProgressMerge.js:314-330](../shared/js/swProfileProgressMerge.js:314)). The reset branch for a queued patch writes about 50 fields back to their defaults ([swProfileProgressMerge.js:333-400](../shared/js/swProfileProgressMerge.js:333)). Terminal and advanced-progress preservation is handled by after-the-fact guards (`preserveTerminalProfileProgress`, `preserveAdvancedProfileProgress`) rather than a schema. Nothing stops a mute-block patch and an unblock-unmute patch from being combined into one record. The entropy plan lists this as deferred. The review did not find a new instance, but this is the same shape.

**S4-3. Likely, medium. The merge base falls back to the module-level progress cache.** When the caller passes no existing record, `normalizeProgressPatch` reads `existing || getProfileNukeProgress()?.[normalizedHref]` ([swProfileProgressMerge.js:324](../shared/js/swProfileProgressMerge.js:324)). `getProfileNukeProgress` returns the module global ([sw.js:1689](../shared/js/sw.js:1689)). That is the partial-or-full cache from S1-1. A row event for an href that the scoped progress omitted therefore merges onto whatever the global holds, which can be an old record. The row-event path passes `previous` explicitly ([sw.js:32791](../shared/js/sw.js:32791)), so this fallback is reached when `previous` is null, which is when the scoped progress lacks the href.

**S4-4. Verified sound in S4:** the row-event path rejects non-queued event kinds, missing items, durable-only items, and terminal rows before writing ([sw.js:32773-32859](../shared/js/sw.js:32773)). It checks attempt identity before applying a milestone ([sw.js:32822](../shared/js/sw.js:32822)). Explicit status changes go through `applyWithinStorageUpdate` with an attempt guard and a row-changed check ([sw.js:32890-32907](../shared/js/sw.js:32890)).

**Checked and withdrawn:** I first suspected that stale-attempt diagnostics are a storage write held under the lock. They are not. `recordStaleAttemptDiagnostic` pushes to a bounded in-memory list ([sw.js:5706-5720](../shared/js/sw.js:5706)).

### Slice S5: queued-tab fill wrapper and loop (sw.js:33964–34124, 34459–35016) — read in full

**S5-1. Confirmed, high impact. A fill timeout abandons the loop, and the loop keeps running.** The fill is wrapped in `promiseWithTimeout(fillQueuedTabsOnce(options), QUEUED_TAB_CREATE_TIMEOUT_MS * 2)` ([sw.js:34032-34036](../shared/js/sw.js:34032)). On timeout the `.finally` clears the in-flight marker through `resetQueuedTabFillState` ([sw.js:34078-34081](../shared/js/sw.js:34078)). The underlying `fillQueuedTabsOnce` is not cancelled. Two things follow:
- A new fill can start while the old loop is still opening tabs. Both loops write the module globals `queuedTabFillOpeningActionKeys` (replaced at fill start, [sw.js:34013](../shared/js/sw.js:34013)) and `queuedTabFillAuthorityState` ([sw.js:34014-34017](../shared/js/sw.js:34014)). The abandoned loop's dedupe keys are then discarded under it.
- The budget is short for the work. Each open may wait up to `QUEUED_TAB_CREATE_TIMEOUT_MS` (10 s) for creation, and then runs `confirmQueuedTabOpenDurableActiveEffect` and the content-script ensure. A fill that opens two or three tabs can take longer than the 20 s budget, so an overlap is plausible. I did not measure how often fills run that long.

The shared reservation counter (`getQueuedProfileOpenReservationCount`) limits some of the damage, which is why this is not rated higher. It is the same shape as S2-1 and S2-2: a timeout is used as a stop signal, but the work it times out keeps running and keeps writing to shared state. Suggested fix: pass an abort token that `fillQueuedTabsOnce` checks at each `await`, and make the timeout path set it. A fill-start test should assert that at most one loop opens tabs at a time.

**S5-2. Likely, medium. The outer error handler can drop a live activation guard.** The loop's outer `catch` runs `deleteActivationGuardByKey(activationGuardKey)` ([sw.js:34997](../shared/js/sw.js:34997)). The `try` that contains the open also contains steps that run after the tab exists: `recordCoordinatorJobProgressViaQueueMachine`, the post-transition confirm, and `confirmQueuedTabOpenDurableActiveEffect` ([sw.js:34915](../shared/js/sw.js:34915), [34945](../shared/js/sw.js:34945), [34947](../shared/js/sw.js:34947)). If one of those throws after `openedTabs` was incremented, the guard is removed and the tab is left without one. The same profile can then be opened again by a later fill. This depends on an unhandled throw from those calls, which I did not confirm.

**S5-3. Likely, low. Fire-and-forget promises without a rejection handler.** `void releaseQueuedTab(tab.id, {...})` at [sw.js:34956](../shared/js/sw.js:34956) has no `.catch`. `void contentEnsurePromise.then(result => handleQueuedTabContentScriptEnsureResult(...))` at [sw.js:34990-34992](../shared/js/sw.js:34990) also has none. A rejection from either becomes an unhandled promise rejection with no diagnostic. `releaseQueuedTab` has no internal catch at its top level that I could see.

**S5-4. Confirmed, low. A single exception stops the whole fill.** The outer `catch` at [sw.js:34996-35005](../shared/js/sw.js:34996) breaks out of the loop, so one bad item ends the fill, and the remaining queued items wait for the next resume (1 s). Nothing records which item failed beyond the `queued-tab-fill-error` reason.

**S5-5. Confirmed, low (pattern 8, timing magic numbers).** The fill loop has literal delays and multipliers: `QUEUED_TAB_CREATE_TIMEOUT_MS * 2` for the fill budget and for `returnAfterDurableActive` ([sw.js:34774-34776](../shared/js/sw.js:34774), [34034](../shared/js/sw.js:34034)), `scheduleOwnedBlockedProfileSweep(..., 750)` ([sw.js:34993](../shared/js/sw.js:34993)), and the 1000 ms resume delays. Named constants exist for some of these. The multipliers are not named.

**S5-6. Verified sound in S5:** the pause check is repeated inside the loop, not only before it ([sw.js:34567](../shared/js/sw.js:34567)). The create attempt carries an `abandoned` flag, so a create that times out closes its own late tab ([sw.js:34772-34773](../shared/js/sw.js:34772), [34808-34809](../shared/js/sw.js:34808)). Reservations are released on every path I traced out of the claim (checked at each `break`, `continue` and `return`). The opening-key set is per fill, so a key blocks duplicate planning only within one fill ([sw.js:34750](../shared/js/sw.js:34750)).

**Systemic note (S2-1, S2-2, S5-1, S3-1):** four separate findings share one shape. A timeout or an error is used as a stop signal, but the work it times out is not cancelled, and it keeps writing to module-level state (`queuedTabConcurrency`, `queuedTabFillOpeningActionKeys`, `coordinatorPauseRequested`, `profileNukeProgress`). A single cancellation token passed through the enqueue, fill and reconcile paths would address the whole group. That is worth more than four separate patches.

### Slice C: content script (shared/js/content.js) — read in part

**C-1. Likely, high impact (wrong account acted on). A remapped queued tab can block or mark a profile other than the queued one.** `getQueuedProfileRemapTarget` returns the page's href when it differs from the queued href ([content.js:9208-9219](../shared/js/content.js:9208)). The href normalizer is shared and canonicalizes `/profile/<slug>` and `/<slug>` to one form ([subject.js:209-236](../shared/js/subject.js:209)), so a remap means a different slug, which is a different account. The action path records "Profile remapped" and then does not return ([content.js:12186-12196](../shared/js/content.js:12186)). It next tests `isProfileBlocked()` against the page it is on. If that is already blocked, it calls `closeQueuedBlockedTab`, which records `already-blocked` with `blockSucceeded: true` under the queued href. That href is never reassigned on this path. If the page is not blocked, the flow goes on to `blockProfile`, which acts on the page's own profile and does not receive the queued href. The worker does have a separate `profile-redirect-mismatch` path ([sw.js:4186-4190](../shared/js/sw.js:4186)), fed by the tab-update listener ([swBootstrap.js:529-540](../shared/js/swBootstrap.js:529)). Nothing orders the two, so the content script can act before the worker closes the tab.

Suggested fix: when remapped, stop before any block, mute or already-blocked decision. Record the terminal state against the page's profile, or terminalize as a redirect mismatch, as the worker already does. Add a test with a page whose slug differs from the queued target, asserting no click and no success attributed to the queued href.

**C-2. Likely, low to medium. A claim received while an action is running is acknowledged as accepted.** The `claim-current-profile-tab-action` handler overwrites `autoProfileAction` and returns `accepted: true` ([content.js:2293-2298](../shared/js/content.js:2293)). It does not check `autoProfileActionPending`. The guard in `maybeRunAutoProfileAction` returns early while an action is pending ([content.js:11452](../shared/js/content.js:11452)). The `finally` that clears the pending flag does not re-run the function ([content.js:12353](../shared/js/content.js:12353)), and nothing re-arms the rescue timer when the action ends. The rescue is re-armed only by `nudgeCurrentProfilePendingClaim`, which init and a few refresh paths call ([content.js:398](../shared/js/content.js:398), [608](../shared/js/content.js:608), [2091](../shared/js/content.js:2091)). The worker's nudge treats `accepted: true` as success and does not resend ([sw.js:28791-28798](../shared/js/sw.js:28791)). So the worker can record a claim as taken by a tab that is still busy with a different action, and the overwritten claim is recovered only by a later nudge or refresh. Suggested fix: reply `accepted: false` with `reason: 'busy'` while pending, so the worker keeps the claim and resends it.

**C-3. Likely, low to medium. Two retry loops have no ceiling.** On profile pages while `coordinatorQueueLoaded` is false, the function re-arms itself every `QUEUED_ACTION_CLAIM_FAST_RETRY_DELAY_MS` (500 ms, [content.js:11456-11473](../shared/js/content.js:11456)). The attempt counter is incremented there but never checked on that branch. The loop stops only if the worker ever returns a `coordinatorQueue`. Each pass calls the content-state refresh, which has its own throttle. The second loop re-arms every 5 s for as long as a pending queue item exists for this profile ([content.js:11488-11499](../shared/js/content.js:11488)). Neither loop has a diagnostic at the point it gives up, because it never gives up. Suggested fix: a ceiling with a recorded diagnostic.

**C-4. Confirmed, latent (pattern 2). A null-regex fallback has no label floor, but the only caller is dead code.** `getGlobalPrimaryAction` requires a label match only when `preferredRegex` is set ([content.js:4576-4600](../shared/js/content.js:4576)). Its comment describes the hijack that the label requirement prevents, and that protection does not cover the null case. The only null-regex caller is `getDialogAction` ([content.js:4287](../shared/js/content.js:4287)), and `getDialogAction` has no callers. The live path, `dismissProfileActionSurface`, always passes a regex ([content.js:12558](../shared/js/content.js:12558)). Suggested fix: delete `getDialogAction`, and add the label floor to the null case as defense in depth.

**C-5. Confirmed, medium (entropy pattern 0 and 6). The progress merge exists twice on the content side and once in the worker.** `normalizeProfileProgressPatch` in `content.js` ([content.js:1019](../shared/js/content.js:1019)) is a second implementation of the worker's `normalizeProgressPatch` ([swProfileProgressMerge.js:314](../shared/js/swProfileProgressMerge.js:314)). It uses a per-tab copy of progress as its base ([content.js:1021](../shared/js/content.js:1021)), which is a third copy of the same data. The two merges must agree on what a patch means, and nothing checks that they do.

**C-6. Confirmed, low (pattern 8). Literal timing values with no names.** The modal handlers use `setTimeout(openModalProfiles, 1e3)` and `2e3` without keeping the timer handle ([content.js:2336](../shared/js/content.js:2336), [2352](../shared/js/content.js:2352)). **C-7. Confirmed, low. The page message protocol uses two discriminators.** `onMessage` dispatches on `request.action` and then on `request.status` ([content.js:2219-2373](../shared/js/content.js:2219)). A message can match either field, and a field typo silently falls through to a no-op.

**C-8. Likely, low to medium (cost on busy pages). The `areExtensionOnlyMutations` guard covers the space page but not the profile-page checks. Not measured.
